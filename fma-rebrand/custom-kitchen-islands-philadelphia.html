What makes an island custom

A custom island is sized for the way your kitchen gets used: extra seating for homework, a second sink for prep, hidden trash and recycling pull-outs, or open shelving for cookbooks and serving pieces.

We start with your layout: walkway clearance, where the range or sink sits, and how the island connects to the rest of the cabinets. Then the island is matched to your cabinet style instead of dropped in as a mismatched box.

Upgrades worth planning for

These are the upgrades homeowners add to an island most often. Decide on them early, because some need electrical or plumbing work before the cabinets go in.

  • Overhang for stool or bar seating
  • A second prep sink or beverage fridge
  • Outlets built into the island base (most kitchen islands need at least one under electrical code)
  • Waterfall countertop edges on one or both sides
  • Trash, recycling and utensil pull-outs
  • Open shelving or a bookcase end panel

Sizing it right

As a rule of thumb, aim for at least 42″ of clearance on every side of an island, and closer to 48″ on the side people cook from. If the kitchen cannot fit that comfortably, a peninsula (an island joined to a wall of cabinets at one end) is often a better fit than squeezing in a full island.

We bring a tape measure to the free walkthrough and tell you straight whether an island makes sense for your space.

Questions

How much does a custom kitchen island cost?

It depends mainly on size, countertop material, and whether you're adding electrical, plumbing or seating. We give exact pricing after seeing your kitchen on the free walkthrough rather than a generic number that won't match your space.

Do I need an electrician for an outlet in my island?

Most kitchen islands need at least one outlet under electrical code. Plan for it early, so the wiring is in before the cabinets and countertop go on, not added after. Tell us on the walkthrough and we'll plan the project around it.

Can you match a new island to my existing cabinets?

We'll match the door style, finish and hardware as closely as possible. If it's close but not exact, we can talk through options on the walkthrough.

What's the difference between an island and a peninsula?

An island stands free on all sides; a peninsula connects to a run of cabinets at one end. Peninsulas work well in smaller kitchens that cannot fit the clearance a full island needs.
